Frequently asked questions

What is compound interest?

It is interest earned on both your original money and on interest from earlier periods — "interest on interest". Over long periods this snowball effect is the main driver of savings growth.

Which formula does this calculator use?

For the starting amount: A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)n×t, where n is the compounding frequency. Monthly contributions are grown month by month at the monthly rate and added on top, so the total combines both parts.

Does compounding frequency make a big difference?

Only a modest one. At 6% over 10 years, daily compounding beats yearly compounding by roughly half a percent of the final value. Your contribution amount and time in the market matter far more.

Are these results guaranteed?

No. The calculator assumes a fixed rate, no taxes, no fees and no withdrawals. Real investments fluctuate and savings rates change, so treat the result as an illustration, not a promise.
