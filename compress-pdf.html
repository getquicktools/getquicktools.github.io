Compress PDF

Shrink a large PDF by re-rendering each page as an image at the quality you choose, then rebuilding the PDF from those images. You can see the original and new size before you download.

🔒 Private by design: your PDF is compressed locally in your browser — it is never uploaded to any server.

How to use

  1. Drop your PDF into the box, or click it to select a file. Its size and page count are shown.
  2. Choose a compression level — Medium is a good balance for most scanned documents.
  3. Click Compress & download and wait while each page is re-rendered.
  4. The new size is shown next to the original so you can see exactly how much was saved.

Frequently asked questions

How does this compressor work?

Each page is rendered to an image in your browser, and a new PDF is built from those images. Because scanned and photo-heavy PDFs store large images, re-encoding the pages as right-sized JPEGs usually makes the file much smaller.

Important: will the text in my PDF still be selectable?

No. Pages are converted to images, so the text is no longer selectable or searchable in the compressed file, and links and form fields are lost. This tool is best for scanned or photo-heavy PDFs. If you need searchable text, this is not the right method.

Is my PDF uploaded to a server?

No. Compression happens entirely in your browser using the pdf.js and pdf-lib libraries, so even confidential documents stay on your device.

My compressed file came out bigger. Why?

If the original PDF already contains efficient text and vector content, turning pages into images can make it larger — especially at the High setting. Try the Medium or Low level, or keep the original file for text-heavy documents.
