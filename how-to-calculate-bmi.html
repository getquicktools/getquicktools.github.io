Good to know

BMI — Body Mass Index — compares your weight to your height: BMI = weight in kilograms divided by height in metres, squared. A 70 kg person who is 1.75 m tall has a BMI of 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) ≈ 22.9, inside the healthy 18.5–24.9 band.

  • BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis — it cannot tell muscle from fat, so muscular people often score 'overweight' unfairly.
  • Measure height without shoes and weight in light clothing for a fair reading.
  • Children and teenagers use age-based BMI percentiles, not the adult categories.

Frequently asked questions

What is BMI?

Body Mass Index is your weight in kilograms divided by the square of your height in metres (kg/m²). It is a quick screening measure used worldwide to classify underweight, normal weight, overweight and obesity in adults.

What are the BMI categories?

Underweight: below 18.5. Normal: 18.5–24.9. Overweight: 25–29.9. Obese: 30 and above. These are the standard World Health Organization ranges for adults.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

BMI is a general guide, not a diagnosis. It can overestimate body fat in muscular athletes and underestimate it in older adults. For a full picture, talk to a healthcare professional.

Does this calculator work for children?

No — children and teenagers use age- and sex-specific BMI percentiles instead of the adult ranges shown here.
