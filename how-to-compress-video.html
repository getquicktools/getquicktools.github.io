How to Compress a Video File

Shrink a video for WhatsApp, email or uploads. Pick a smaller resolution and quality level, and get a compact MP4 — processed entirely on your own device.

Good to know

Phone videos are huge — a minute of 4K footage can weigh hundreds of megabytes, far over chat and email limits. Compressing re-encodes the video at a smaller size so it sends quickly and plays everywhere, at a modest cost in sharpness.

  • For chats and social media, 720p looks great on phones and is a fraction of 4K's size.
  • Compress the original recording, and keep the original file for your archive.
  • Very long videos take time to process in a browser — for a quick share, trim first on your phone, then compress.

Frequently asked questions

How can a browser compress video without a server?

The page loads ffmpeg — the industry-standard video tool — compiled to WebAssembly (ffmpeg.wasm). It runs entirely on your device, which is why nothing is uploaded and why big files take a while: your phone or computer does all the work a server would normally do.

What do the quality settings (CRF) mean?

CRF is the H.264 quality scale: lower numbers mean better quality and bigger files. 23 is high quality, 28 is a balanced default, and 32 squeezes the file much smaller with visibly softer detail. Combined with a lower resolution, it is the fastest way to shrink a video for messaging apps.

Will the resolution setting make my video bigger?

No. The scale step only ever shrinks: if your video is already smaller than the chosen height, it is kept as it is rather than upscaled.

Why is it slow, and why do you recommend under 200 MB?

The browser holds the whole file in memory and encodes with a single processing thread, so long or very large videos can take minutes and may run out of memory on phones. For files over ~200 MB, a desktop app will be far quicker and more reliable.
