Good to know

Extracting text is how you quote a report in your own document, search a long file for a keyword, or feed a PDF's contents into another tool. If the PDF was made from a text document, its words come out clean and complete.

  • Scanned PDFs are pictures of pages — they contain no text layer, so nothing can be extracted from them without OCR.
  • Check the extracted text around tables and columns: reading order can differ from the visual layout.
  • Extract first, then use the Word Counter to check the length of what you pulled out.

Frequently asked questions

Is my PDF uploaded to a server?

No. The text is extracted entirely in your browser using the pdf.js library, so even confidential documents stay on your device.

My result is empty. Why?

Some PDFs — typically scans and photos of documents — contain no text layer at all, only images of pages. This tool reads the built-in text layer only; OCR (reading text from page images) is not included, so scanned PDFs need an OCR tool instead.

Why does the layout look different from the PDF?

A PDF stores text as positioned fragments, not flowing paragraphs. The extractor joins the fragments in reading order and keeps line breaks where the PDF has them, so columns and tables may come out in a different visual order than on the page.

Can I extract text from a password-protected PDF?

Encrypted or password-protected PDFs cannot be read by the browser library. Remove the password in your PDF viewer first, then extract.
