Good to know

A QR code is just information drawn as a pattern: point a phone camera at it and the link, text or contact opens. Shops put them on counters for menus and payments, and they beat typing a long web address every time.

  • Test your code with your own phone before printing it — one wrong character breaks the link.
  • Print with strong contrast (dark code on light background) and leave a quiet border around it.
  • Bigger is better at a distance: a code scanned from a metre away should be at least 3–4 cm wide.

Frequently asked questions

Do QR codes from here expire?

No. These are static QR codes that encode your text directly, so they work forever as long as the content (for example the website address) stays the same.

Can I use the QR code commercially?

Yes — the PNG you download is yours to use on menus, flyers, products and business cards at no cost.

How much text can a QR code hold?

Up to roughly 4,000 characters, but shorter is better: short links scan faster and more reliably, especially when printed small.

Why won't my QR code scan?

Make sure there is enough contrast and a quiet margin around the code, and avoid shrinking a large-code image too small. Test with your phone before printing in bulk.
