Good to know

Forms and platforms are picky: a profile photo must be square, a banner a wide strip, an ID photo an exact size in pixels. Resizing gives you those exact dimensions instead of hoping an upload form accepts whatever you have.

  • Keep the aspect ratio locked unless you deliberately want to stretch the image.
  • Shrinking is safe; enlarging a small image much bigger will look soft — start from the largest original you have.
  • Common sizes: 1080×1080 for square social posts, 1200×630 for link previews, passport photos follow your country's official spec.

Frequently asked questions

What does "lock aspect ratio" mean?

It keeps the image's original proportions. If you change the width, the height is recalculated automatically so the picture never looks stretched or squashed. Turn it off only when you deliberately need an exact width and height.

Can I make an image bigger without losing quality?

Enlarging adds no new detail — the browser stretches the existing pixels, so big upscales can look soft. For modest increases (up to about 150%) the result is usually fine for web use.

Does resizing keep the same file format?

Yes — a JPG stays a JPG, a PNG stays a PNG, and WebP stays WebP whenever your browser can encode it. The estimated size shown is the actual size of the resized file, calculated before you download.

Is there a size limit?

There is no upload limit because nothing is uploaded — the practical limit is your device's memory. Extremely large images may be slow on older phones.
